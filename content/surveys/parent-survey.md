# Lobby — Discovery surveys


Two instruments, one per tab: a written survey for parents (about 10 minutes) and a read-aloud interview for kids aged 7–12 (about 15 minutes). Together they test the decisions in the decision doc before any of them are built.

## 1. About your family

1. How many kids do you have between 5 and 14? For each, their age.
2. Which one are you thinking of most when you answer this survey? (Age.) *Answer the rest for that kid.*
3. Where do they mostly watch YouTube? (Pick up to two.) iPad · iPhone · TV · Mac or laptop · Someone else's device · School Chromebook
4. Which YouTube are they on? Full YouTube on a channel under my Google account · Full YouTube on their own supervised account · Full YouTube, logged out · YouTube Kids · Not sure
5. Do you have a Mac at home you use regularly? Yes · No
6. Do you use Apple Family Sharing? Yes · No · Not sure

## 2. YouTube today

7. Roughly how much YouTube do they watch on a school day? Under 30 min · 30–60 min · 1–2 hours · More than 2 hours · No idea
8. Name up to three channels or creators they watch most. *(Leave blank if you can't.)*
9. How confident are you that the list above is right? Very · Somewhat · I'm guessing
10. In the last month, has your kid said or done something from YouTube you didn't understand? (A phrase, a joke, a dance, a reference.) Yes, often · Once or twice · No
11. What did you do about it? Asked them · Looked it up · Asked another parent · Let it go · Other: \_\_\_
12. Have you tried any of these? (Pick all.) YouTube Kids · Supervised account settings · Screen Time limits · Blocking specific channels · Taking the device away · Watching with them · None
13. For each one you tried: did it help, and did it last? (Free text, one line each.)
14. What's the last video or creator you watched *together* on purpose? (Free text. "Never" is a fine answer.)

## 3. What you actually want

*These are forced choices on purpose. Pick the one closer to you even if neither is exact.*

15. If you could have only one: know what they're watching · limit what they're watching
16. If you could have only one: a weekly summary of their YouTube world · a live view of what they're watching right now
17. If you could have only one: block the stuff you don't like · get your own stuff into their feed
18. When your kid watches something you think is junk, what bothers you most? That they're watching it at all · That I don't know what it is · That I can't compete with it · That it's taking time from other things
19. Rank these from most to least important to you: (1) knowing what they watch, (2) understanding what it means, (3) shaping what they see, (4) limiting how much they watch, (5) having things to talk about with them
20. Complete the sentence: "I'd feel like I was doing my job if I could \_\_\_." (Free text.)
21. Complete the sentence: "The thing I'd never want my kid to find out I could see is \_\_\_." (Free text; "nothing" is allowed.)

## 4. Reactions to five ideas

*Rate each 1–5 (1 = wouldn't use, 5 = I'd use this every week), then one line on why.*

22. **A weekly note** about your kid's YouTube: the five or six creators that were most of their week and what each one is, the memes explained, what changed since last week, and two questions you could ask. You see creators and topics, not a video-by-video list.
23. **Add**: you subscribe their channel to a creator you like, or put a video in their Watch Later, and it shows up in their normal YouTube. They know it came from you.
24. **Ask about this**: you tap a creator or a meme and it becomes a conversation prompt, not a block. There is no block.
25. **Watch together**: either of you flags a video, and it becomes a thing to do tonight.
26. **Their side**: your kid can see which of your channels and interests you've shared, and a plain-language screen of what you can and can't see about them.
27. The weekly note arrives a week behind, never live, and never sends notifications. Does that make it: more trustworthy · less useful · both · doesn't matter
28. If the note showed you which channels came to your younger kid via an older sibling, would that be: useful · none of my business · not applicable
29. Which of the five ideas would you delete without missing it? (Pick one or "none.")
30. Which one would you pay for on its own? (Pick one or "none.")

## 5. Logistics and price

31. To set this up, you'd export your kid's YouTube history once from Google (about five minutes, on a computer). Would you do that? Yes · Yes, if you walk me through it · No
32. Would you do it again each week to keep the note current? Yes · Monthly at most · No, it has to be automatic
33. If keeping it current meant leaving a Mac app signed in to your Google account, would you? Yes · Only if I understood exactly what it reads · No
34. What do you currently pay for, per month, that's for your kids? (Pick all.) Streaming · Games or Roblox · Learning apps · Screen-time or safety apps · Nothing
35. What does a parental-control or safety app cost, in your head? (Free text, a number.)
36. Would you rather pay: monthly · yearly · once
37. Is there a price per year at which this would feel like an obvious yes? (A number, or "no.")
38. Would you let me talk to your kid for fifteen minutes about YouTube, with you in the room? Yes · No
39. Anything I didn't ask that I should have?

## What each question tests

| Questions | Decision it tests | Result that changes the feature set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3–5 | Brand accounts and Mac companion as the data path | Under half on brand accounts or under half with a Mac: the iOS Takeout import is the primary path, not the fallback |
| 8–9 | The Edition's core value: parents don't know the creators | If most parents name the top three confidently, the Edition needs to lead with meaning, not identity |
| 10–11 | The translation job is real | Few "yes, often": the glossary is secondary; lead with creators and presence |
| 12–13 | Why prior tools failed | If "watching with them" is the one thing that lasted, watch-together moves up |
| 15–19 | Control vs understanding vs presence | If "limit" and "block" win the forced choices, the demand is for a control app; the add-never-remove rule is a niche, not the market |
| 20–21 | What parents feel is their job, and where surveillance starts for them | Q21 answers name the line the parent view must not cross |
| 22–26, 29–30 | Each surface's standalone worth | The idea most would delete is cut from v1; the one most would pay for on its own is the launch pitch |
| 27 | Delay as the privacy setting | Majority "less useful": the delay is a real cost, revisit cadence |
| 28 | Sibling reporting | Majority "none of my business": drop it |
| 31–33 | Takeout friction and the Mac's signed-in path | Weekly re-export refused by most: the automated Mac path is load-bearing, not optional |
| 34–37 | Price anchor | Q35 sets the ceiling parents already have in their head; Q37 gives the yearly number |

The survey never asks "would you buy this." Q30 and Q37 are as close as it gets, and the pilot is the real answer.
