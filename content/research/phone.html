For parents of 7-to-12-year-olds

Your kid's YouTube is a whole world. You should get to visit.

We're building something for parents who want to understand what their kids watch, and be part of it, without blocks, timers, or spying. Before we build it, we want to hear from you. And, with your permission, from them.

Take the parent survey Run the kid interview

YouTube Kids didn't stick.

Most kids over six reject it as babyish. So they end up on full YouTube, on a channel under your account or on yours, and the algorithm gets to know them better than you do.

Every tool on offer is a fence.

Blocks, timers, supervised settings. They limit how much. None of them tell you what any of it means, or give you a way in.

Kids this age still care what you think.

That window closes around twelve. Until then, a parent's interest counts for more than any setting. We think that's the thing to build on.

What we're exploring

Not another control app. Something closer to a translator, and a shared space.

A weekly note that explains the creators and memes that made up your kid's week, in plain language, so "what is skibidi toilet" has an answer before dinner.

A way to put something you love into their feed, with your name on it, instead of quietly removing what you don't.

A way to turn "what is that" into a question you ask them, rather than a block they route around.

Whether any of that is right is what these two surveys are for.

I was an engineer at Apple for seventeen years, and I have three kids, ages seven to ten. Between them they've shown me more of YouTube than I ever chose to see. I'm not trying to get it out of their lives. I'm trying to stop it being a place I can't follow them into. If you feel the same, ten minutes of your time will shape what I build, and I'll share what I learn with everyone who takes part.

Helen Ma
Founder, Studio Ikigai · parent of three

How we handle answers
No accounts. No tracking scripts. Nothing sold. We never ask your child's name. Answers are used for one thing: deciding what to build. If you leave an email, it's only so we can invite you to the pilot.
